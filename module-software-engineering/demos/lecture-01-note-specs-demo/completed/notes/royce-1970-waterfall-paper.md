# Royce 1970 Waterfall Paper

Royce's 1970 paper "Managing the Development of Large Software Systems" is
cited everywhere as the origin of the waterfall model. Reading it is a
surprise: the famous single-pass diagram appears early — and then the paper
spends most of its remaining pages explaining why that approach does not
work.

## Summary

The paper cited as waterfall's origin actually calls the single-pass
approach risky and prescribes feedback between phases and building a pilot
version first. Its reputation and its content disagree — a caution about
descriptions drifting away from the thing they describe.

## What Royce actually said

Royce writes that the unmodified sequence of analysis-then-coding "is risky
and invites failure," because testing sits at the end of the process, where
trouble is most expensive. The bulk of the paper is corrective steps: feed
information backward between phases, involve the customer continuously, and
document relentlessly.

### The "do it twice" caveat

His most striking prescription: if the software is novel, build a throwaway
pilot version first — "do it twice" — so that the delivered second version
rests on evidence instead of guesses.

## My take

The paper filed under "origin of waterfall" is an argument for iteration and
feedback. The citation-graph version of the idea and the written-down version
of the idea disagree — which is a lesson about specifications all by itself.

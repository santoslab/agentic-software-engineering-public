## Essence vs. accident

Brooks divides the difficulties of software into essence — the inherent
complexity of the conceptual construct itself — and accident — the
difficulties of expressing that construct in a given technology. His claim:
no single technique will yield an order-of-magnitude gain, because most of
the remaining difficulty is essential.

## The four essential difficulties

- Complexity: software has more distinct states than anything else humans build.
- Conformity: software must bend to arbitrary interfaces designed by people.
- Changeability: successful software is pressured to change forever.
- Invisibility: software has no natural geometric representation to inspect.

## Relevance to agentic software engineering

Coding agents look like an attack on accident: they generate build mechanics,
boilerplate, and translations between representations at remarkable speed.
The essence — deciding what the software should be, which is the work of
specification — appears to remain squarely human.
